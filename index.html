About

A Neighbor Who
Knows This Place

I've called Union Township home since 1987, and my family goes back generations here. My husband and I met back in grade school, and together we've planted and grown our own roots here while raising our kids. I come from blue-collar, service-minded people, and that's shaped how I see this work. But this path is mine to tell.

I started working at fifteen at Deep River Waterpark and never really stopped. Nannying, restaurants, sales, whatever kept things moving while I finished my Business Administration degree at Ivy Tech and Purdue Northwest and built a career in marketing and business development for small local entrepreneurs. I became a mom in 2015. Around that same time my grandmother, a longtime secretary for U.S. Steel, was diagnosed with Parkinson's. I became her primary caregiver until she passed in 2017. My youngest was born in 2022. Through all of it I kept working, kept learning, kept showing up. That is just what you do.

I got into real estate in January 2020 and watched the housing market change in real time. That sent me back to school for my Master of Public Administration at Claremont Lincoln University. I did not want just an opinion. I wanted the tools to understand what was actually happening and what could be done about it. I finished that degree while raising three kids and running a business.

I did not inherit this path. I built it one job and one degree at a time and I am still building. Some families in this township are doing fine. Others are counting every dollar. This office has to work for both and I know what both feel like. I am proud to run as a Democrat and have voted that way most of my life. But this job has never really been about that. It is about showing up for your neighbors and doing the work right.

Platform

What I'm Here to Do

This office serves every household in the township — the ones doing just fine right now, and the ones feeling every bill. That work isn't Democrat or Republican. It's just work that has to get done well.

Housing and Community Access

Township assistance exists for residents facing housing instability, utility shutoffs, and medical hardship — most people don't know it's there, whether they've never needed it or need it right now. I'll make sure they do, and make the process simple enough that qualifying for help isn't a job in itself.

Environmental Responsibility

The wetlands and green spaces here belong to the people who live here, not to whoever's next in line with a development proposal. Responsible stewardship means planning for the next generation, not just the next approval cycle.

Open and Honest Government

This office manages public money for every resident, regardless of how they vote. That means plain-language communication, records anyone can actually find, and a door that's open in practice — not just on a sign.

Civic Knowledge and Connection

People make better decisions when they understand the systems that affect them. Part of this job is making sure residents know what they're entitled to, what their rights are, and how to actually use this office.

A Voice for This Township

Union Township is small, but that's no reason for it to get overlooked. I'll speak up for this township in the county and state-level rooms where the decisions that actually affect us get made — not just in our own meetings.

The Office

What the Trustee
Is Responsible For

Most residents have never had a reason to know what this office does — that's worth changing. The Township Trustee is the closest point of government to where people actually live, and the responsibilities are more concrete, and more consequential, than most people realize.

  • Township Assistance — Emergency help for residents in genuine need: shelter, utilities, food, medical expenses, burial costs. If you or someone you know needs it, this program exists and you have a right to ask for it.
  • Cemetery Stewardship — Maintaining township cemeteries, including abandoned and neglected burial grounds, as an act of respect for the people who came before us.
  • Fire Protection Oversight — Making sure fire protection services are in place and the township is fully covered.
  • Township Property — Managing and maintaining township-owned land and facilities on behalf of every resident.
  • Budget Administration — Stewarding public funds carefully and accounting to every taxpayer in the township, regardless of politics.
  • Community Navigation — Being someone residents can actually reach when they need help understanding their options, finding resources, or figuring out which door to knock on first.

Before Election Day

Make Sure You're
Ready to Vote

This all runs through the state, not this campaign — so it's the same regardless of who you're voting for. Takes about two minutes to check.

Register or Check Your Status

Register to vote, confirm you're already registered, or update your address at indianavoters.in.gov. Registration for the November election closes October 5, 2026.

Find Your Polling Place

Your polling location can change between elections. Look yours up at indianavoters.in.gov, or call 1-866-IN-1-VOTE if you'd rather talk to a person.

Key Dates

Registration closes October 5. Early voting begins October 6. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
